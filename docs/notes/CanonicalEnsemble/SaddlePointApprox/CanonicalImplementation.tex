\section{Overview}

The \texttt{ThermalModelCanonical} class computes thermodynamics of a hadron resonance gas (HRG) in the canonical ensemble (CE) with exact conservation of baryon number~$B$, electric charge~$Q$, strangeness~$S$, and optionally charm~$C$.
The CE partition function reads
\begin{align}
  Z(\bN) &= \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\!\frac{d\phi_B}{2\pi}
             \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\!\frac{d\phi_Q}{2\pi}
             \int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\!\frac{d\phi_S}{2\pi}\;
             e^{\,g(\bphi)} \,,
  \label{eq:ZCE}
\end{align}
where $\bN = (B,Q,S,\ldots)$ are the conserved charges and
\begin{align}
  g(\bphi) &= -i\,\bN\!\cdot\!\bphi
              + \sum_j \sum_{n=1}^{n_\mathrm{max}} z_j^n\,
                e^{\,i\,n\,\bq_j\cdot\bphi} \,.
  \label{eq:gdef}
\end{align}
Mean multiplicities and covariances are obtained from partition function ratios $R(\bc) \equiv Z(\bN - \bc)/Z(\bN)$:
\begin{align}
  \langle N_j\rangle_\CE
  &= \sum_{n} n\,z_j^n\;R(n\,\bq_j) \,,
  \label{eq:Nce}\\
  \covv_\CE(N_j, N_k)
  &= \delta_{jk}\sum_n n^2\,z_j^n\,R(n\,\bq_j)
    + \sum_{n,m} nm\,z_j^n\,z_k^m\,
      \Big[R(n\,\bq_j + m\,\bq_k) - R(n\,\bq_j)\,R(m\,\bq_k)\Big] \,.
  \label{eq:cov_exact}
\end{align}

Three methods are available, selected via \texttt{SetMethod()}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\texttt{CanonicalMethod} & Value & Description \\
\midrule
\texttt{GaussLegendre}  & 0 & Multi-dimensional numerical quadrature \\
\texttt{SaddlePoint}     & 1 & Per-quantum-number saddle-point solve \\
\texttt{SaddlePointNLO}  & 2 & Analytic LO+NLO from CGF expansion \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}


\section{Gauss--Legendre quadrature (\texttt{GaussLegendre})}
\label{sec:GL}

The multi-dimensional Fourier integral~\eqref{eq:ZCE} is evaluated by composite 10-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature on $[0,\pi]^d$ ($d$ = number of canonical charges).
The interval for each angle is divided into $n_\mathrm{max}$ subintervals, with
\begin{align}
  n_\mathrm{max} = m \times \max\!\Big(3,\;\sqrt{B^2+Q^2+S^2+C^2}\Big) \,,
\end{align}
where $m \geq 1$ is a user-adjustable multiplier (\texttt{SetIntegrationIterationsMultiplier()}).

\paragraph{Baryon analytical integration.}
When enabled (\texttt{m\_Banalyt}), the baryon fugacity $\phi_B$ is integrated analytically using modified Bessel functions, reducing the quadrature dimension by one:
\begin{align}
  \int_0^\pi \frac{d\phi_B}{\pi}\;\cos(B_g\,\phi_B)\;\exp\!\Big(2|w|\cos(\phi_B - \psi)\Big)
  = I_{B_g}(2|w|) \,.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Saddle-point contour shift.}
When the baryon analytical path is \emph{not} active (\texttt{m\_Banalyt = false}), the code shifts the Fourier integration contour through the saddle point before evaluating the GL quadrature.
Concretely, \texttt{PrepareModelGCE()} solves the GCE constraint equations
\begin{align}
  \sum_j \sum_n n\,q_{a,j}\,\omega_j^{*,n} = N_a
  \qquad (a \in \text{canonical charges})
\end{align}
for the saddle-point chemical potentials $\bmu_\mathrm{sp}$, and \texttt{FillChemicalPotentials()} propagates them to each species.
This is equivalent to the contour deformation $\phi_a \to \phi_a + i\mu_{\mathrm{sp},a}/T$ (Cauchy's theorem for periodic analytic functions) and is mathematically exact.

The effect is to populate the antisymmetric multiplicities $\texttt{Nsy}[\bq] = V_c\sum_{j:\bq_j=\bq}\sinh(\mu_j/T)\,z_j$ in the integrand (Eq.~\ref{eq:gdef}).
At the saddle point, the condition $\sum_{\bq} q_a\,\texttt{Nsy}[\bq] = N_a$ ensures that the linear phase term $-N_a\,\phi_a + \sum_{\bq} q_a\,\texttt{Nsy}[\bq]\sin(q_a\,\phi_a)$ vanishes at $\bphi = 0$ to first order.
This eliminates the rapid oscillation that occurs at finite chemical potentials (e.g.\ strangeness-canonical at nonzero~$\mu_B$), where the large $\texttt{Nsy}$ terms from strange baryons cause severe cancellation in the integrand.

The multiplicative prefactor $e^{-\bN\cdot\bmu_\mathrm{sp}/T}$ introduced by the contour shift cancels implicitly: in the partition function ratios $R(\bc) = Z(\bN-\bc)/Z(\bN)$, it leaves a residual factor $e^{\bc\cdot\bmu_\mathrm{sp}/T}$ that is exactly compensated by the shifted GCE densities $n_j^{\GCE}(\bmu_\mathrm{sp})$ already used in $\langle N_j\rangle_\CE = R(\bq_j)\,n_j^{\GCE}$.
For entropy, the shift contributes $\ln Z(\bN) = -\bN\cdot\bmu_\mathrm{sp}/T + \ln\tilde{Z}(\bN)$; the additional term $-\bmu_\mathrm{sp}\cdot\bN/(T\,V_c)$ combines with the shifted thermal contributions to reproduce the correct canonical entropy.
No explicit prefactor correction is needed for any thermodynamic observable.

The contour shift is \emph{not} applied in the \texttt{m\_Banalyt} mode.
The Bessel identity used there assumes particle--antiparticle symmetry ($\texttt{Nsx}[\bq] = \texttt{Nsx}[-\bq]$), which breaks at $\bmu \neq 0$.
This restriction is benign: \texttt{m\_Banalyt} is active only when all four charges are canonical, where the integrand at $\bmu = 0$ is already symmetric under $\bphi \to -\bphi$ and has no oscillation problem.

\paragraph{Outputs.}
The quadrature produces $Z(\bN-\bc)$ for all needed charge vectors~$\bc$.
The ratios $R(\bc)$ are then fed into Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Nce}--\eqref{eq:cov_exact} to obtain means and covariances.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
At large correlation volumes ($V_c \gtrsim 5000$~fm$^3$), catastrophic cancellation in double precision degrades the fluctuation observables: $R(\bc+\bc')/[R(\bc)\,R(\bc')]$ deviates from unity by only $\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$ but both numerator and denominator are $\sim e^{\mathcal{O}(V_c)}$.
The saddle-point contour shift eliminates the oscillation problem at finite~$\bmu$ but does not resolve the peak-narrowness issue at very large volumes: the integrand peak width scales as $\sim 1/\sqrt{N_\mathrm{tot}}$, which can be too narrow for the GL quadrature to resolve even with the contour shift.
In this regime, the saddle-point methods (Sections~\ref{sec:SP1}--\ref{sec:SP2}) should be used instead.

\section{Saddle-point approximation (\texttt{SaddlePoint})}
\label{sec:SP1}

This method applies the Gaussian saddle-point approximation to each partition function individually, then forms ratios.

\subsection{Saddle-point ingredients}

Deform the contour $\phi_a = \mu_a/T + i\theta_a$ and define:
\begin{align}
  \omega_j^{*,n} &\equiv z_j^n\,\exp\!\Big(n\,\bq_j\cdot\frac{\bmu^*}{T}\Big) \,,
  \label{eq:omega}
\end{align}
where $\bmu^*/T$ solves the saddle-point (GCE constraint) equations:
\begin{align}
  \sum_j \sum_n n\,q_{a,j}\,\omega_j^{*,n} = N_a \,,
  \qquad a \in \{B,Q,S,\ldots\} \,.
  \label{eq:saddle}
\end{align}
The \emph{susceptibility matrix} is
\begin{align}
  \Sigma_{ab} = \sum_j q_{a,j}\,q_{b,j}\,W_j^{(2)} \,,
  \qquad
  W_j^{(k)} \equiv \sum_{n} n^k\,\omega_j^{*,n} \,.
  \label{eq:Sigma}
\end{align}

\subsection{Partition function ratios}

For each charge vector~$\bc$, solve the shifted saddle-point equations for $\bmu_\bc^*$ with charges $\bN - \bc$ and compute the ratio as
\begin{align}
  \boxed{
  \ln R(\bc)
  = \frac{V_c}{T}\big[p(\bmu_\bc^*) - p(\bmu_0^*)\big]
  - \bigg[\sum_a \frac{\mu_{\bc,a}^*(N_a-c_a)}{T} - \sum_a \frac{\mu_{0,a}^* N_a}{T}\bigg]
  - \frac{1}{2}\big[\ln\det\Sigma_\bc - \ln\det\Sigma_0\big] \,,
  }
  \label{eq:SP1_lnR}
\end{align}
where $p(\bmu) = \sum_j p_j(T,\mu_j^*)$ is the total GCE pressure and $\Sigma_\bc$ is evaluated at $\bmu_\bc^*$.
Each of the three terms is $\mathcal{O}(1)$, avoiding the catastrophic cancellation that plagues GL at large~$V_c$.

\subsection{Algorithm}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:saddle} for $\bmu^*_0$ and compute $p_0$, $\Sigma_0$, $\ln\det\Sigma_0$.
  \item For each $\bc \neq 0$: solve the shifted equations via Broyden's method (warm-started from the previous solution), compute $p_\bc$, $\Sigma_\bc$, and evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:SP1_lnR}.
  \item Feed the $R(\bc)$ values into Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Nce}--\eqref{eq:cov_exact}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Conservation laws.}
Mean charges $\sum_j q_{a,j}\langle N_j\rangle_\CE$ deviate from~$N_a$ by $\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$, since the Gaussian saddle-point for each $R(\bc)$ is evaluated independently.


\section{Saddle-point NLO (\texttt{SaddlePointNLO})}
\label{sec:SP2}

This method computes means and covariances directly from the saddle-point expansion of the cumulant generating function (CGF), bypassing partition function ratios entirely.
See the companion note~\cite{SaddlePointNote} for full derivations.

\subsection{Key quantities}

At the saddle point $\bmu^*/T$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:saddle}), define:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Cluster moments: $W_j^{(k)} = \sum_n n^k\,\omega_j^{*,n}$ \quad ($k = 1,2,3,4$).
  \item Susceptibility matrix: $\Sigma_{ab} = \sum_j q_{a,j}\,q_{b,j}\,W_j^{(2)}$.
  \item Projection: $G_{jk} = \bq_j^T\,\Sigma^{-1}\,\bq_k$ \quad [$\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$].
  \item Response matrix: $D_{jm} = \delta_{jm} - W_m^{(2)}\,G_{jm}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mean yields (LO + NLO)}

\begin{align}
  \boxed{
  \langle N_l\rangle_\CE
  = W_l^{(1)}
    - \frac{1}{2}\,W_l^{(3)}\,G_{ll}
    + \frac{1}{2}\,W_l^{(2)}\sum_j W_j^{(3)}\,G_{jj}\,G_{jl}
    + \mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1}) \,.
  }
  \label{eq:mean}
\end{align}
The first term is the GCE mean [$\mathcal{O}(V_c)$].
The second and third are the NLO canonical correction [$\mathcal{O}(V_c^0)$], involving the third cluster moment~$W^{(3)}$ (quantum-statistical skewness).

\subsection{Covariances (LO + NLO)}

\begin{align}
  \covv_\CE(N_l, N_m)
  = \underbrace{\delta_{lm}\,W_l^{(2)} - W_l^{(2)}\,W_m^{(2)}\,G_{lm}}_{\text{LO: } \mathcal{O}(V_c)}
  \underbrace{- \frac{1}{2}\,B_{lm} + \frac{1}{2}\,C_{lm}}_{\text{NLO: } \mathcal{O}(1)}
  + \mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1}) \,,
  \label{eq:cov}
\end{align}
where the NLO terms involve auxiliary quantities:
\begin{align}
  C_{lm} &= \Tr(M_l\,M_m) \,, \quad
  (M_l)_{ca} = \sum_j W_j^{(3)}\,D_{jl}\,P_{j,c}\,q_{a,j} \,,
  \quad P_{j,a} = \sum_b (\Sigma^{-1})_{ab}\,q_{b,j} \,,
  \label{eq:Clm}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
  B_{lm} &= \sum_j G_{jj}\Big[W_j^{(4)}\,D_{jl}\,D_{jm}
  + W_j^{(3)}\,\frac{\partial^2\tilde{\alpha}_j}{\partial\alpha_l\partial\alpha_m}\bigg|_0\Big] \,,
  \label{eq:Blm}
\end{align}
with the second-derivative response
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial^2\tilde{\alpha}_j}{\partial\alpha_l\partial\alpha_m}\bigg|_0
  = -\sum_a q_{a,j}\sum_b (\Sigma^{-1})_{ab}\,S_{b,lm} \,,
  \qquad
  S_{b,lm} = \sum_k q_{b,k}\,W_k^{(3)}\,D_{kl}\,D_{km} \,.
  \label{eq:d2alpha}
\end{align}

\subsection{Thermodynamic quantities (LO + NLO)}
\label{sec:thermo}

Bulk thermodynamic quantities---pressure, energy density, and entropy density---follow from derivatives of $\ln Z(\bN)$ with respect to the thermodynamic variables.
We derive each in turn, starting from the saddle-point expression for the canonical partition function.

\subsubsection{Starting point: saddle-point $\ln Z$}

After performing the Gaussian integral around the saddle point $\bmu^*/T$, the canonical partition function is
\begin{align}
  \ln Z(\bN) = \frac{V_c}{T}\,p^{\GCE}(\bmu^*) - \sum_a \frac{\mu_a^* N_a}{T}
  - \frac{d}{2}\ln(2\pi) - \frac{1}{2}\ln\det\Sigma + \mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1}) \,,
  \label{eq:lnZ_SP}
\end{align}
where $p^{\GCE}(\bmu^*) = T\sum_j \sum_n \omega_j^{*,n}/V_c$ is the GCE pressure at the saddle-point chemical potentials, $d$ is the number of canonically conserved charges with nonzero susceptibility (i.e.\ $d = \mathrm{rank}(\Sigma)$), and $\Sigma_{ab}$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Sigma}.

\paragraph{Stationarity.}
The saddle-point condition~\eqref{eq:saddle} ensures $\partial(\ln Z)/\partial\alpha_a^* = 0$ (where $\alpha_a \equiv \mu_a/T$).
This means that when differentiating $\ln Z$ with respect to external parameters ($T$, $V_c$), the implicit dependence $\bmu^*(T, V_c)$ drops out, and only the \emph{explicit} dependence on $T$ and $V_c$ contributes.
This is the key simplification.

\subsubsection{Pressure}

The canonical pressure is
\begin{align}
  P = T\,\frac{\partial \ln Z(\bN)}{\partial V_c}\bigg|_T \,.
\end{align}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:lnZ_SP}, the $V_c$-dependent terms are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V_c\,p^{\GCE}/T$: Since $\omega_j^{*,n} \propto V_c$ at fixed $\alpha_a^* = \mu_a^*/T$, the GCE pressure $p^{\GCE}$ is $V_c$-independent, so $\partial(V_c\,p^{\GCE}/T)/\partial V_c = p^{\GCE}/T$.
  \item $-\sum_a \mu_a^* N_a/T$: By stationarity, this is constant (the saddle-point $\bmu^*$ does not change with $V_c$ at fixed $\alpha^* = \mu^*/T$, and $N_a$ is fixed).
  \item $-\tfrac{1}{2}\ln\det\Sigma$: Since $\Sigma_{ab} = \sum_j q_{a,j}\,q_{b,j}\,W_j^{(2)}$ and each $W_j^{(2)} = \sum_n n^2\,\omega_j^{*,n} \propto V_c$ at fixed $\alpha^*$, we have $\Sigma = V_c\,\hat\Sigma$ where $\hat\Sigma$ is $V_c$-independent. Thus $\ln\det\Sigma = d\ln V_c + \ln\det\hat\Sigma$, and $\partial\ln\det\Sigma/\partial V_c = d/V_c$.
\end{itemize}
Combining:
\begin{align}
  \boxed{
  P = p^{\GCE}(\bmu^*) - \frac{d\,T}{2\,V_c} + \mathcal{O}(V_c^{-2}) \,.
  }
  \label{eq:pressure}
\end{align}
The NLO correction $-dT/(2V_c)$ is purely geometric (it counts the number of conserved-charge constraints) and is independent of the particle content.

\subsubsection{Energy density}

The canonical energy density is
\begin{align}
  \varepsilon = \frac{T^2}{V_c}\,\frac{\partial \ln Z(\bN)}{\partial T}\bigg|_{V_c} \,.
  \label{eq:eps_def}
\end{align}
We differentiate Eq.~\eqref{eq:lnZ_SP} with respect to $T$ at fixed $V_c$, using stationarity to ignore the implicit $T$-dependence through $\bmu^*(T)$.
Writing $\alpha_a^* = \mu_a^*/T$, the ``fixed $\alpha^*$'' derivatives are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\partial(V_c\,p^{\GCE}/T)/\partial T\big|_{\alpha^*} = V_c\,\varepsilon^{\GCE}/T^2$, from the identity $T^2\,\partial(p/T)/\partial T\big|_{\alpha} = \varepsilon$ (derived below, Eq.~\ref{eq:dpoverTdT}).
  \item $\partial(-\sum_a \alpha_a^* N_a)/\partial T\big|_{\alpha^*} = 0$ (fixed).
  \item $-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\partial\ln\det\Sigma/\partial T\big|_{\alpha^*} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Tr(\Sigma^{-1}\,\partial\Sigma/\partial T\big|_{\alpha^*})$.
\end{itemize}
The first two terms give the LO contribution $\varepsilon^{\GCE}(\bmu^*)$.
The NLO correction requires computing $\partial\Sigma/\partial T\big|_{\alpha^*}$.

\paragraph{Temperature derivative of $\Sigma$.}
Since $\Sigma_{ab} = \sum_j q_{a,j}\,q_{b,j}\,W_j^{(2)}$ and $W_j^{(2)} = \sum_n n^2\,\omega_j^{*,n}$, we need $\partial\omega_j^{*,n}/\partial T\big|_{\alpha^*}$.
From the definition~\eqref{eq:omega}, $\omega_j^{*,n} = V_c\,p_{j,n}/T$, where $p_{j,n}$ is the $n$-th cluster contribution to the GCE pressure of species~$j$.
The standard cluster expansion gives
\begin{align}
  p_{j,n} = \frac{(\pm 1)^{n+1}}{n}\,\frac{d_j\,T}{2\pi^2}\,m_j^2\,K_2\!\Big(\frac{n\,m_j}{T}\Big)\,e^{n\mu_j/T}
  = (\pm 1)^{n+1}\,p_j^{\mathrm{Boltz}}\!\Big(\frac{T}{n},\,\mu_j\Big) \,,
  \label{eq:pjn}
\end{align}
where the second equality holds because the Boltzmann pressure at temperature $T/n$ has a prefactor $d_j\,(T/n)/(2\pi^2) = d_j\,T/(n\cdot 2\pi^2)$, which precisely absorbs the cluster-expansion factor $1/n$.
This is what \texttt{DensityCluster($n$, Pressure)} returns.

We define $\varepsilon_{j,n}$ through the same relation used for the total pressure:
\begin{align}
  \varepsilon_{j,n} \equiv T^2\,\frac{\partial(p_{j,n}/T)}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha} \,.
  \label{eq:epsjn_def}
\end{align}
Using the identity (proved below) $T^2\,\partial(p/T)/\partial T\big|_\alpha = \varepsilon$, this gives
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial\omega_j^{*,n}}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha^*}
  = \frac{V_c}{T^2}\,\varepsilon_{j,n}(\mu_j^*) \,.
  \label{eq:domega_dT}
\end{align}
One can verify that $\varepsilon_{j,n} = (\pm 1)^{n+1}\,\varepsilon_j^{\mathrm{Boltz}}(T/n,\,\mu_j)$, which is what \texttt{DensityCluster($n$, EnergyDensity)} returns (see below).

\paragraph{Proof of the identity $T^2\,\partial(p/T)/\partial T\big|_\alpha = \varepsilon$.}
One might naively expect the entropy density here, since $\partial p/\partial T|_\mu = s$.
The key is that we differentiate $p/T$ (not~$p$) and hold $\alpha = \mu/T$ fixed (not~$\mu$).
Using $\mu = \alpha T$ and the chain rule:
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha}
  = \frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\mu} + \frac{\partial p}{\partial \mu}\bigg|_{T}\,\frac{\mu}{T}
  = s + \frac{n\mu}{T} \,,
\end{align}
where $s$ and $n$ are the entropy and number densities.
Then:
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial(p/T)}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha}
  = \frac{1}{T}\frac{\partial p}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha} - \frac{p}{T^2}
  = \frac{Ts + n\mu - p}{T^2}
  = \frac{\varepsilon}{T^2} \,,
  \label{eq:dpoverTdT}
\end{align}
where the last step uses the Euler relation $\varepsilon + p = Ts + \mu\,n$.

\paragraph{Proof that $\varepsilon_{j,n} = (\pm 1)^{n+1}\,\varepsilon_j^{\mathrm{Boltz}}(T/n,\,\mu_j)$.}
From Eq.~\eqref{eq:pjn}, $p_{j,n}/T = (\pm 1)^{n+1}\,p_j^{\mathrm{Boltz}}(T/n,\,\mu_j)/T$.
Let $\tilde{T} = T/n$, so $T = n\tilde{T}$.
At fixed $\alpha = \mu/T$, the ratio $\mu/\tilde{T} = n\alpha$ is also constant.
Hence $\partial/\partial T\big|_\alpha = (1/n)\,\partial/\partial\tilde{T}\big|_{\mu/\tilde{T}}$ and
\begin{align}
  T^2\,\frac{\partial(p^{\mathrm{Boltz}}(\tilde{T},\mu)/(n\tilde{T}))}{\partial T}\bigg|_\alpha
  = \frac{n^2\tilde{T}^2}{n}\cdot\frac{1}{n}\,\frac{\partial(p^{\mathrm{Boltz}}/\tilde{T})}{\partial\tilde{T}}\bigg|_{\mu/\tilde{T}}
  = \tilde{T}^2\,\frac{\partial(p^{\mathrm{Boltz}}/\tilde{T})}{\partial\tilde{T}}\bigg|_{\mu/\tilde{T}}
  = \varepsilon^{\mathrm{Boltz}}(\tilde{T},\mu) \,,
\end{align}
where the last equality uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:dpoverTdT} for a Boltzmann gas at temperature~$\tilde{T}$.
Therefore $\varepsilon_{j,n} = (\pm 1)^{n+1}\,\varepsilon_j^{\mathrm{Boltz}}(T/n,\,\mu_j)$, confirming that \texttt{DensityCluster($n$, EnergyDensity)} directly gives~$\varepsilon_{j,n}$.

Therefore:
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial W_j^{(2)}}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha^*}
  = \sum_n n^2\,\frac{\partial\omega_j^{*,n}}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha^*}
  = \frac{V_c}{T^2}\sum_n n^2\,\varepsilon_{j,n}(\mu_j^*) \,.
  \label{eq:dW2dT}
\end{align}
Define the \emph{energy-weighted cluster sum}:
\begin{align}
  \boxed{
  E_j \equiv \sum_{n=1}^{n_\mathrm{max}} n^2\,\varepsilon_{j,n}(\mu_j^*) \,.
  }
  \label{eq:Ej}
\end{align}
The $n^2$ factor arises because $W_j^{(2)} = \sum_n n^2\,\omega_j^{*,n}$, so differentiating brings down $n^2$ times $\partial\omega/\partial T$.
For Boltzmann statistics only $n=1$ contributes, giving $E_j = \varepsilon_j^{\GCE}(\mu_j^*)$.

\paragraph{Trace formula.}
Substituting into the susceptibility derivative:
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial\Sigma_{ab}}{\partial T}\bigg|_{\alpha^*}
  = \sum_j q_{a,j}\,q_{b,j}\,\frac{V_c\,E_j}{T^2} \,,
\end{align}
and using $G_{jk} = \bq_j^T\Sigma^{-1}\bq_k$:
\begin{align}
  \Tr\!\Big(\Sigma^{-1}\,\frac{\partial\Sigma}{\partial T}\Big\rvert_{\alpha^*}\Big)
  = \sum_j G_{jj}\,\frac{V_c\,E_j}{T^2} \,.
\end{align}
The NLO energy correction is thus
\begin{align}
  \delta\varepsilon = -\frac{T^2}{2V_c}\,\Tr\!\Big(\Sigma^{-1}\,\frac{\partial\Sigma}{\partial T}\Big\rvert_{\alpha^*}\Big)
  = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_j E_j\,G_{jj} \,,
\end{align}
giving the full result:
\begin{align}
  \boxed{
  \varepsilon = \varepsilon^{\GCE}(\bmu^*)
  - \frac{1}{2}\sum_j E_j\,G_{jj}
  + \mathcal{O}(V_c^{-2}) \,,
  }
  \label{eq:energy}
\end{align}
where $\varepsilon^{\GCE}(\bmu^*) = \sum_j \varepsilon_j(T,\mu_j^*)$ is the GCE energy density at the saddle point.

\subsubsection{Entropy density}

The canonical entropy density follows from the exact statistical mechanics identity (no chemical potential term in the canonical ensemble since the charges are fixed):
\begin{align}
  \boxed{
  s = \frac{\ln Z(\bN)}{V_c} + \frac{\varepsilon}{T} \,.
  }
  \label{eq:entropy}
\end{align}
This holds at any volume.
Here $\ln Z(\bN)$ is given by the saddle-point expression~\eqref{eq:lnZ_SP}, already computed and stored in \texttt{m\_MultExp}.
The energy density $\varepsilon$ is given by Eq.~\eqref{eq:energy}.
No separate NLO formula is needed---the entropy is automatically consistent through $\ln Z$ and $\varepsilon$.

\paragraph{Euler relation.}
The Euler relation $P = Ts - \varepsilon$ (equivalently $PV_c = T\ln Z$) holds only in the thermodynamic limit $V_c \to \infty$.
At finite~$V_c$, the deviation $s - (\varepsilon + P)/T = \ln Z/V_c - P/T$ is $\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$.
This can be verified explicitly: using Eq.~\eqref{eq:lnZ_SP},
\begin{align}
  \frac{\ln Z}{V_c} = \frac{p^{\GCE}}{T} - \frac{\bmu^*\cdot\bN}{TV_c} - \frac{d\ln(2\pi)}{2V_c} - \frac{\ln\det\Sigma}{2V_c} \,,
\end{align}
while $P/T = p^{\GCE}/T - d/(2V_c)$, so the difference is $\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$ as expected.

\subsubsection{Numerical validation}
\label{sec:nlo_validation}

The NLO corrections to pressure and energy density have been validated numerically by comparing against exact Gauss--Legendre (GL) quadrature results at moderate volumes.

\paragraph{Method.}
For a range of correlation volumes $V_c$, the GL method (Section~\ref{sec:GL}) provides numerically exact results (up to quadrature error).
At large enough~$V_c$, all thermodynamic densities admit a $1/V_c$ expansion:
\begin{align}
  f(V_c) = f_\LO + \frac{f_{\NLO}}{V_c} + \mathcal{O}(V_c^{-2}) \,.
\end{align}
The NLO coefficient $f_{\NLO}$ can be extracted from GL data via linear regression of~$f$ against $1/V_c$.
Independently, the analytic NLO prediction is obtained from two \texttt{SaddlePointNLO} calculations at well-separated volumes $V_1$ and $V_2$:
\begin{align}
  f_\NLO^\mathrm{(analytic)} = \frac{V_1\,V_2\,\big[f(V_1) - f(V_2)\big]}{V_2 - V_1} \,.
\end{align}

\paragraph{Results: $d = 3$ (no charmed hadrons).}
Using the PDG2020 particle list without charmed hadrons at $T = 155$~MeV, $\bN = 0$, with canonical $B$, $Q$, $S$, $C$ conservation and Boltzmann statistics, GL calculations at $V_c = 500$--$2000$~fm$^3$ with integration multiplier~$m = 3$ give:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & GL slope & NLO slope & Ratio & Analytic \\
\midrule
$\varepsilon$ & agrees & agrees & $\approx 0.99$ & --- \\
$P$           & $\approx -0.233$ & $\approx -0.233$ & $\approx 0.99$ & $-3T/2 = -0.2325$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The pressure slope matches the analytic prediction $-dT/2$ with $d = 3$, corresponding to $\mathrm{rank}(\Sigma) = 3$: although charm is formally canonical, the particle list contains no charmed hadrons, so $\Sigma_{CC} = 0$ and the charm direction does not contribute.

\paragraph{Results: $d = 4$ (with charmed hadrons).}
Switching to the particle list with charmed hadrons and setting a large charm fugacity $\gamma_C \gg 1$ to ensure $\Sigma_{CC}\,V_c \gg 1$, the GL pressure slope at $V_c = 200$--$1000$~fm$^3$ gives $\approx -0.297$, consistent with the analytic prediction $-4T/2 = -0.310$.
At physical $\gamma_C = 1$, where charm production is thermally suppressed ($\Sigma_{CC}\,V_c \ll 1$), the GL slope reverts to $\approx -0.240$, closer to $d = 3$.

\paragraph{Effective dimensionality.}
The parameter $d$ in the NLO pressure formula~\eqref{eq:pressure} is the number of charge directions for which the saddle-point (Gaussian) approximation is valid.
The Gaussian approximation for charge~$a$ requires that the integration range $[-\pi, \pi]$ be much wider than the Gaussian width $\sigma_a \sim 1/\sqrt{\Sigma_{aa}\,V_c}$, i.e.\
\begin{align}
  \Sigma_{aa}\,V_c \gg 1 \,.
  \label{eq:validity}
\end{align}
When this condition is violated (as for charm at physical $\gamma_C$), the Gaussian extends well beyond $[-\pi, \pi]$ and the saddle-point approximation breaks down for that direction.
The NLO formula then overestimates the correction from that charge: it predicts a $-T/(2V_c)$ contribution per charge, but the true correction is exponentially small when $\Sigma_{aa}\,V_c \to 0$.
In practice, the effective dimensionality is $d = \mathrm{rank}_\mathrm{eff}(\Sigma\,V_c)$, counting only the directions satisfying~\eqref{eq:validity}.

\paragraph{Entropy limitation.}
The NLO entropy density~\eqref{eq:entropy} inherits a limitation from the saddle-point $\ln Z$ expression~\eqref{eq:lnZ_SP}.
The NLO terms in $\varepsilon$ and~$P$ are correctly captured because they arise from derivatives of $\ln Z$ with respect to $T$ and $V_c$, which naturally select the $\mathcal{O}(V_c^0)$ and $\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$ contributions.
However, $\ln Z$ itself contains an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ NNLO correction (from the next term in the saddle-point expansion) that is not computed.
Since $s = \ln Z/V_c + \varepsilon/T$, this missing $\mathcal{O}(1)$ term in $\ln Z$ produces an $\mathcal{O}(V_c^{-1})$ error in~$s$.
This is a limitation of both the \texttt{SaddlePoint} and \texttt{SaddlePointNLO} methods---they share the same saddle-point $\ln Z$---and does not affect $\varepsilon$ or~$P$.

\subsection{Algorithm}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:saddle} for $\bmu^*/T$ via Broyden's method.
  \item Compute $W_j^{(k)}$ ($k = 1,\ldots,4$), the GCE energy density $\varepsilon^{\GCE}(\bmu^*)$ and pressure $p^{\GCE}(\bmu^*)$, and the energy-weighted cluster sums~$E_j$ for all species from the cluster expansion.
  \item Build $\Sigma_{ab} = \sum_j q_{a,j}\,q_{b,j}\,W_j^{(2)}$ directly from the $W^{(2)}$ values.  Invert to get $\Sigma^{-1}$.
  \item Compute $G_{jj}$, $P_{j,a}$, and $D_{jl}$ for all species.
  \item Evaluate NLO thermodynamic quantities from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:pressure}--\eqref{eq:energy}.
  \item Evaluate means from Eq.~\eqref{eq:mean}: cost $\mathcal{O}(N_\mathrm{spec}^2)$.
  \item If fluctuations are requested, evaluate the full covariance matrix from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:cov}--\eqref{eq:d2alpha}: cost $\mathcal{O}(N_\mathrm{spec}^3)$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Conservation laws.}
The identity $\sum_l q_{a,l}\,D_{jl} = 0$ ensures $\sum_l q_{a,l}\,\langle N_l\rangle_\CE = N_a$ exactly, order by order.
Similarly, $\varr_\CE(Q_a) = 0$ is satisfied at LO by $\Sigma - \Sigma\,\Sigma^{-1}\,\Sigma = 0$.


\section{Selective canonical treatment}

Any subset of charges can be treated canonically while the rest remain grand-canonical.
For example, canonical $B$ and $S$ with grand-canonical $Q$: the saddle-point integral becomes two-dimensional, with a $2\times2$ susceptibility submatrix.
All formulas hold with $\Sigma^{-1}$ restricted to the canonical subspace and charge vectors projected accordingly.

\paragraph{When to use which.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GaussLegendre}: Small to moderate volumes ($V_c \lesssim 100$--$1000$~fm$^3$) or when the saddle-point approximation is not reliable.
    The saddle-point contour shift enables use at finite~$\bmu$ for partial canonical ensembles.
    Increase the multiplier $m$ for better precision.
  \item \textbf{SaddlePoint}: Large volumes where GL suffers from cancellation.
    Best when exact partition function ratios $R(\bc)$ are needed (e.g.\ for higher-order cumulants beyond covariances).
  \item \textbf{SaddlePointNLO}: Large volumes when exact conservation of mean charges matters (e.g.\ thermal fits).
    Fastest for fluctuations. Does not compute individual $R(\bc)$ values.
\end{itemize}
